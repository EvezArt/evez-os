# EVEZ Rights Strategy

## Operating principle

Do not license rights that the project cannot prove it owns.

The rights system therefore separates:

    ownership evidence
    declared license
    third-party obligations
    commercial opportunity

A file without a recognized license is not assumed to be owned or freely
relicensable. It becomes a review item.

## Existing declared model

Repository materials currently declare MIT for EVEZ-OS and MIT-0 for several
EVEZ-authored skill cards. Other materials declare Apache-2.0, CC-BY-NC-4.0, or
no usable license declaration. Those categories must remain distinct.

## Commercial activation paths

The opportunity ledger can identify:

- hosted software
- enterprise integration
- commercial support
- training and certification
- documentation products
- media licensing
- brand/trademark licensing
- data licensing
- commercial licensing of newly created proprietary modules
- API/service subscriptions

These are candidate business models, not guaranteed revenue.

Existing MIT or other permissive grants generally cannot be retroactively
converted into a more restrictive license for copies already distributed under
the original terms.

## Intellectual property boundary

Patent, trademark, publicity, data-rights, and third-party content questions
are not settled merely because a source file has an SPDX identifier. Those
rights require their own evidence and, where appropriate, legal review.

## Runtime policy

The runtime may:

    detect
    classify
    score
    inventory
    propose
    generate documentation

It may not silently:

    assert ownership
    grant third-party rights
    register a trademark
    file a patent
    promise revenue
    represent legal advice as legal advice
